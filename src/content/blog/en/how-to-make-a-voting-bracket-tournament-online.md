Best pizza topping? Song of the year? Favorite character from the show? Arguments like these end fast with a **voting bracket**: the options face off in pairs, each person picks one per matchup, and the winner moves on until there is a champion. It is the same single-elimination bracket you know from sports, except the voters decide who advances.

In this guide you will make one on QuestioFlux for free and send the link to your friends so they can vote from their phones.

## How the bracket works

- You pick **8 or 16 options**. With 8 there are 7 matchups (quarterfinals, semifinals and final); with 16 there are 15, starting with the round of 16. With QuestioPlus you can also make 32- or 64-option brackets (31 or 63 matchups), great for streams and videos.
- Each option can have **a name, an image or both**. Option images are free.
- Every play gets a **freshly shuffled bracket**, so the matchups change from one person to the next.
- At the end, each person sees **their champion** and its road to the title, with every opponent it beat.
- The results tab shows **everyone's favorites**: how many titles each option won and what share of its matchups it took.

Voters don't need an account and don't even have to type a name: they open the link and start picking.

## Step by step

### 1. Open the bracket maker

Go to the [favorites tournament](/en/create/experiences/tournament) page. There is a playable example you can try first. Start from scratch with **Create a tournament**, or use **Use this template** to copy the example (a pizza bracket) into a draft of your own. You need a free account to create one.

### 2. Fill in the cover

Title, description and category. A question works well as the title: "Which pizza is the best?", "What is the best movie of the decade?". In the description, say what people are choosing: "the tastiest one", "the one you would rewatch".

### 3. Pick 8 or 16 options and fill each one in

Under **Number of options**, pick the size: 8 and 16 are free; 32 and 64 need QuestioPlus or a one-time pass. Then write each option's name (up to 80 characters) and, if you like, upload a JPG, PNG, WEBP or GIF image up to 4 MB.

Images make a big difference when the topic is visual: food, characters, places, cars, album covers. If two options end up with the same name and no image, the editor warns you, because they could face each other.

### 4. Publish it as public or private

While it is a draft, only you can open it. When you publish, choose:

- **Public:** it shows up on your profile and in Explore, and anyone can play.
- **Private:** only people with the link can play. Good for a group chat, a class or an internal vote.

### 5. Send the link

Drop it in the group chat, post it in your stories or pin it to your profile. Everyone votes at their own pace, on a phone or a computer.

## Ready-made example: "Best Thanksgiving side dish"

Eight options to copy: mashed potatoes, stuffing, mac and cheese, green bean casserole, sweet potato casserole, cranberry sauce, cornbread and dinner rolls. Add a photo of each and the bracket is ready in a few minutes.

One way to play it with friends:

1. Everyone plays and posts a screenshot of their champion in the group chat.
2. After a day, open the results tab and announce the overall favorite.
3. Whoever has the champion furthest from the group's favorite picks the next bracket's topic.

## Tips for a bracket people finish

- **Keep options at the same level.** If one option is obviously better, it wins every matchup and the result gets boring.
- **Start with 8.** Seven matchups take a minute. Save 16 for topics everyone has strong opinions about.
- **Images on all options or on none.** Half with photos and half without makes the matchups lopsided.
- **Short names.** In a quick matchup, people need to get the option at a glance.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do voters need to sign up?**
No. Anyone with the link plays as a guest and doesn't even have to enter a name.

**Does everyone get the same bracket?**
No. The bracket is shuffled again on every play, so the matchups change and the overall result is fairer.

**Can I change the options after publishing?**
Yes. If you switch from 16 options to 8, the last ones are deleted, and the editor asks you to confirm first.

**Can I see who voted for what?**
The results tab shows how each option performed across all completed plays. Each person sees their own champion at the end of the bracket.
