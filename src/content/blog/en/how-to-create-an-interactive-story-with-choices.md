Remember choose-your-own-adventure books, where you decide what the character does and flip to another page? An **interactive story** works the same way, on a phone: every scene ends with a few choices, every choice leads to a different scene, and the reader reaches one of the endings you wrote. Then they can start over and try another path.

In this guide you will write a branching story like that on QuestioFlux, with no code at all, and share it by link.

## How it works

- The story is made of **scenes**. Each scene has text (up to 1,200 characters) and up to **4 choices**.
- Each choice **leads to** another scene or to an **ending**.
- Paths can **merge**: two different choices can lead to the same scene, and a choice can even go back to an earlier one.
- Every ending has its own **title** and text, shown in the reader's result.
- A story holds up to **60 scenes**, endings included.

At the end, readers see the ending they reached, how many choices they made and how many endings the story has. On the story page, you see what share of plays reached each ending.

## Before you write: sketch the map

A branching story is much easier to write when you sketch the paths first, on paper or in a list. Decide:

1. **The opening:** who the character is and what the problem is.
2. **The endings:** two or three are enough for a first story. At least one good and one bad ending give the choices weight.
3. **The decision points:** where the story splits and where paths come back together.

Merging paths is the trick that keeps a story from exploding. If every scene had 2 choices and none ever merged, you would have 32 paths to write after just 5 steps.

## Complete example: "The lost map"

Seven scenes, three of them endings:

| Scene | What happens | Choices and destination |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 (start) | You find an old map in your grandmother's attic. An X marks a spot in the woods. | "Follow the map now" → 2 · "Show the map to your grandmother" → 3 |
| 2 | In the woods, the trail splits near a river. | "Follow the river" → 4 · "Climb the hill" → Ending B |
| 3 | Your grandmother says the map belonged to your great-grandfather and asks you to be careful. | "Head to the woods" → 2 · "Put the map in a drawer" → Ending C |
| 4 | At the end of the river, a chest sits half buried. | "Open the chest" → Ending A · "Go back to the fork" → 2 |
| Ending A | **Heir to the map.** Inside the chest, your great-grandfather's letters tell the family story. | (ending) |
| Ending B | **Lost on the hill.** From the top you can see everything except the way back. | (ending) |
| Ending C | **The map in the drawer.** Some adventures wait for another day. | (ending) |

Notice two things: scene 3 leads back to scene 2 (the paths merge), and scene 4 lets readers return to the fork. It is only seven scenes, but there are several ways to reach each ending.

## Step by step on QuestioFlux

### 1. Open the story maker

Go to the [interactive story](/en/create/experiences/story) page, which has a playable example. Start from scratch with **Create a story**, or use **Use this template** to copy the example (a mysterious forest with three endings) into a draft of your own and edit from there. You need a free account to create one.

### 2. Fill in the cover

Title, description and category. In the description, set up the opening and the character's challenge without giving away the endings.

### 3. Write the first scene and its choices

The first scene is the **Start**. Write the text and add the choices. For each choice, the **Leads to** field lists your existing scenes plus **+ New scene** and **+ New ending**, which create the destination on the spot.

### 4. Keep going until every path ends

Fill in each new scene. To close a path, tick **This scene is an ending** and give the ending a title. The scene text becomes what happens in that ending.

### 5. Check the list and publish

Before you publish, the editor lists what needs fixing:

- choices with no destination or no text;
- scenes from which no path reaches an ending (readers would be stuck in a loop);
- whether no ending can be reached from the start;
- scenes nobody can reach, as a warning.

Once everything checks out, publish it as **public** (profile and Explore) or **private** (only people with the link) and send the link.

## Writing tips

- **Choices that change something.** "Open the door" or "Escape through the window" beats "Go left" or "Go right".
- **Short scenes.** Two or three sentences per scene keep the pace up, especially on a phone.
- **Named endings.** The ending title is what readers tell their friends: "I got Heir to the map".
- **There are no variables or inventory.** The path is what changes the story. If an item matters, write separate scenes for readers who picked it up and readers who didn't.
- **Play every path before you share it.** Start over until you have seen each ending at least once.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to know how to code?**
No. You write the scenes and pick where each choice leads. The site handles the rest.

**How many endings can a story have?**
As many as you like, within the 60-scene limit. The story needs at least one ending that can be reached from the start.

**Can two choices lead to the same scene?**
Yes. Paths can merge, and a choice can even go back to an earlier scene.

**Can I edit it after publishing?**
Yes. Changes go live as soon as you save.
